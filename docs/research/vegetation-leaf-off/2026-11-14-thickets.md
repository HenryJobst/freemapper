# Thickets in point clouds flown without leaves (measurement, 2026-11-14)

Question: with which thresholds does the vegetation from laser point clouds (ADR 0105) catch thickets without
turning open forest green? The Bavarian flights are winter flights, and thickets came out as runnable forest
(405). The user asked for a measurement before any change. Its result is ADR 0138.

## Method

A build made for the measurement wrote, for every 3 m cell of the classification, how many returns fall into
twelve height bands, beside the class freemapper gives. It ran over ten example areas with the stored terrain
model, the building mask and the classes each provider leaves out, as `derive` does. A script read the cells,
gave them classes by the rules of `cell_class` (the same class as freemapper in 99.9 to 100 % of the cells) and
then by other rules. Every share below is counted after the majority filter of `derive`. Neither the build nor
the scripts are in the repository.

The flight day of a tile comes from the GPS times of its points. The dates in the names of the cached files are
days of publication.

## The reference

An image-based surface model flown in leaf shows the canopy of the summer. Two areas have one: the Schmausenbuck
(DOM20 of the LDBV, pictures of 2025-06-13) and the Rauener Berge (bDOM of the LGB, pictures of 2024-05-12).
Berlin's bDOM was flown on 2026-03-05, without leaves; the Saarland charges for its image model.

- **Thicket**: the median of the summer canopy height of a cell lies between 3 and 8 m, nowhere in it above 10 m,
  the same in its eight neighbours, and the winter laser has returns above 1.8 m. Schmausenbuck: 711 cells
  (0.64 ha). Rauener Berge: 46 cells, by their profile a pole stand (38 % of the returns at 3 to 8 m, 6 % at 0.8
  to 3 m).
- **Tall forest**: the canopy of a cell and its neighbours is at least 15 m high. Schmausenbuck: 63 238 cells.
  Rauener Berge: 106 087 cells.

The reference is not a survey in the terrain. It says where a low closed canopy stands in summer, not how well
one runs there, and it cannot see what grows below tall trees.

## Why lower thresholds alone fail

Where the returns of the Schmausenbuck lie, in per cent:

| | Ground | 0.2 to 0.8 m | 0.8 to 3 m | 3 to 8 m | Above 8 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thicket | 47 | 3 | 35 | 13 | 3 |
| Tall forest | 32 | 1 | 3 | 6 | 58 |

The index of ADR 0105 counted a return above 3 m with 0.1. In tall forest that alone gives an index of about 0.2
(median 0.20), with nothing growing below the trees; a leafless thicket reaches about 0.3 (median 0.28). Asked
how often a thicket cell has the higher value than a tall-forest cell, the index answers 63 % (50 % would be
chance). Without the share of the crowns the answer is 88 %, and 89 % for the returns between 0.8 and 3 m among
all returns below 3 m, called the density here.

## What the measurement does not show

- Whether the green patches below tall forest are young growth or mistakes. Only their number is known, and it
  falls with the density.
- Pole stands, with crowns at 3 to 8 m and a clear stem space, stay runnable forest under every rule tried. They
  need a third value, the number of stems for example.
- Berlin and Saarland have no reference. The Saarland cloud, thinned to one point per 0.5 m, gives about 30
  returns per cell and scattered patches under every rule.
- On the steep slopes of Cochem the green share rises from 18 to 44 % (density 0.25). Scrub and abandoned
  vineyards make that plausible; nobody checked it.
- The undergrowth classes 407 and 409 were not measured. They come in 0.1 to 1 % of the cells.

The user had no forest known from the terrain to check a candidate against and chose the density with 0.20.
